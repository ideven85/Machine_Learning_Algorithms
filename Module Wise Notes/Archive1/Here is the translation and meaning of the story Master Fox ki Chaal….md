Master Fox's Trick  
  
He used his wits and relied on trickery rather than brute strength. He noticed a bright circle of moonlight right beneath the tree. The Fox went over, stood there, and began a bizarre and strange dance.  
  
He would wave his long tail in the air and spin around in circles like a top. Sometimes he would jump into the air, and other times he would lie flat on the ground, pretending to be dead.  
  
Hypnosis and the Consequence  
  
The Partridges (Teetar) had never seen such a unique spectacle before. Completely mesmerized (hypnotized), they stared at him without blinking. The Fox increased his speed. He was spinning so fast that, as the Partridges watched him continuously, their heads began to spin.  
  
They lost their senses and forgot that they were perched high on a tree branch. Because of the dizziness, their grip on the branch loosened. Suddenly, one Partridge lost its balance and fell to the ground with a thud.  
  
The Fox did not stop his dance, ensuring the other birds wouldn't get distracted. In moments, the other hypnotized Partridges also started falling to the ground, one by one.  
  
Climax  
  
As soon as the last Partridge fell, the Fox stopped his dance and swiftly gathered his prey. The story ended in an instant. Master Fox enjoyed his feast and, with the glint of victory in his eyes, vanished into the darkness of the jungle.

> This story teaches us that we should never get so lost in an outside spectacle or attraction that we forget our own safety.

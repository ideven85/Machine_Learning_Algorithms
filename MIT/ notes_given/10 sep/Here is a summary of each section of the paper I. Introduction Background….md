Here is a summary of each section of the paper: I. Introduction Background on PCA: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a popular technique used to reduce data dimensionality, eliminate noise, and mitigate overfitting by identifying orthogonal directions of maximum variance. PCA in Neural Networks (PCA-NN): Preprocessing inputs with PCA decorrelates features and helps condition the Hessian matrix. This optimizes gradient updates, stabilizes backpropagation, and speeds up convergence, leading many to train neural networks on principal components. Limitations: Traditional PCA-NN preprocessing causes information loss, harms feature interpretability, and severely complicates explainable AI (XAI) pipelines due to the additional transformation step. Proposed Solution: The authors introduce Principal Components-based Initialization (PCsInit), which embeds PCA straight into the weights of a network's first layer, alongside two variants—PCsInit-Act (adds activation functions) and PCsInit-Sub (uses subsets of data for computational efficiency). II. Motivation: Explainability When PCA is Applied Before a Neural Network Perturbation-based XAI: Methods like LIME and Occlusion are less effective in PCA-NN setups because modifying input features disrupts the underlying SVD or covariance structure. Mapping changes back via a PCA loading matrix introduces inaccuracies due to discarded variance and non-invertibility. Gradient-based XAI: While methods like Saliency Maps and Grad-CAM can map gradients back to the original input space using the chain rule, the prior dimensionality reduction can remove or distort fine-grained features necessary for high-quality visualization. Feature Attribution XAI: Methods like SHAP and LIME must calculate values in the PCA-transformed space and back-project them using a potentially non-invertible matrix, making the explanations mere approximations. The PCsInit Advantage: Incorporating PCA straight into the network's initialization enables XAI methods to function seamlessly as they would on a regular neural network, without needing back-projection steps. III. Methodologies **PCsInit Core Idea: Recognizing that the matrix multiplication in PCA ($XW_r$) mimics the linear multiplication of a neural network layer, PCsInit initializes the first layer's weights directly with the extracted principal components and omits the activation function for that layer. Training Protocol (Algorithm 1): The PCA projection matrix ($W_r$) is assigned to the first layer's weights. This layer is initially frozen for $n_{frozen}$ epochs to let deeper layers adapt to the PCA feature space before it is unfrozen for joint fine-tuning.** PCsInit-Sub (Algorithm 2): To combat heavy computational costs on large datasets, this variant extracts principal components from only a subset of the data before assigning weights. PCsInit-Act (Algorithm 3): This variant applies a non-linear activation function right after the PCA-initialized first layer to increase the network's capacity to learn complex, non-linear patterns. Theoretical Analysis: Through Theorem 1, the authors mathematically prove that for a linear regression problem with a single-layer neural network, PCA drops small eigenvalues from the Hessian matrix of the MSE loss function, reducing its condition number and guaranteeing faster gradient descent convergence. IV. Experiments Setup: The strategies were evaluated across 5-layer networks on four binary classification datasets: Heart, Ionosphere, Micromass, and Parkinson. Models were evaluated against three distinct weight setups (He, Xavier, and Orthogonal initialization) over 200 epochs. Performance: Across the datasets, PCsInit and its variants outperformed standard neural networks (NN) and matched or exceeded traditional PCA-NN in testing accuracy and convergence speed. Variant Comparisons: PCsInit-Sub demonstrated robustness and competitive accuracy while successfully reducing computational overhead and training time on larger datasets. PCsInit-Act proved highly stable and emerged as the top-performing approach on three out of four datasets when combined with Xavier initialization. V. Conclusion Summary of Work: The authors confirm that PCsInit, PCsInit-Sub, and PCsInit-Act simplify model explainability while yielding superior optimization, faster convergence, and better accuracy compared to traditional NN and PCA-NN pipelines. Future Work: Future research will explore these initialization strategies across different neural network architectures, test them against noisy or imbalanced datasets, and investigate using Kernel PCA in place of standard PCA.

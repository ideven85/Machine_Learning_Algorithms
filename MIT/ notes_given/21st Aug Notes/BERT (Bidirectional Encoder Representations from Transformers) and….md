**BERT** (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) and **MAE** (Masked Autoencoders) are both self-supervised learning methods that use a "mask-and-predict" strategy. **BERT applies this to language (text), while MAE applies it to computer vision (images)**. [++[1](https://www.meta-intelligence.tech/en/insight-self-supervised-learning#:~:text=Reviewing%20the%20core%20narrative:%20*%20Breaking%20the,on%20the%20internet;%20the%20success%20of%20BER)++, ++[2](https://docs.nvidia.com/tao/tao-toolkit/6.25.10/text/cv_finetuning/pytorch/self_supervised_learning/mae.html#:~:text=Introduction.%20Masked%20Autoencoders%20(MAE)%20are%20a%20self%2Dsupervised,visual%20representations%20without%20the%20need%20for%20labeled)++, ++[3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-EBqzYIJRaQ#:~:text=and%20telling%20that%20the%20sentence%20is%20positive,for%20the%20language%20models%20for%20the%20co)++]   
  
**Core Concept Similarities **  
* **The Goal**: Both train a model by hiding parts of the input data and forcing the model to guess what is missing.   
* **The Backbone**: Both typically use a Transformer architecture to process input tokens or patches.   
* **Self-Supervision**: Both learn from massive amounts of unlabeled data instead of relying on human-labeled datasets. **Key Differences **  
* **Data Domain**: BERT works on text (words or sub-words); MAE works on images (pixel patches).   
* **Information Density**: Language has high semantic density (hiding one word changes a lot). Images have high spatial redundancy (neighboring pixels look alike).   
* **Masking Ratio**: BERT masks about 15% of words. MAE masks a huge portion of an image, usually 75% to 90%, because pixels are redundant.   
* **Architecture Efficiency**: BERT processes only the unmasked tokens (which is a small amount in text anyway). MAE takes this idea further for images: its heavy encoder processes only the 25% visible patches, while a lightweight decoder handles the missing patches, making MAE very fast and scalable.
